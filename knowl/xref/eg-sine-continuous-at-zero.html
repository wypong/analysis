Example 4.1.8.

The sine function is continuous at \(0\text{.}\) Measure angles in radians. For \(0 \lt h \lt \pi/2\text{,}\) let \(O\) be the center of the unit circle, \(A=(1,0)\text{,}\) and \(P=(\cos h,\sin h)\text{.}\) Let \(Q=(\cos h,0)\text{,}\) and let the ray \(OP\) meet the tangent line at \(A\) at \(T\text{.}\)
🔗
The length \(PQ=\sin h\) is at most the chord length \(PA\text{,}\) which is at most the arc length \(h\text{.}\) Thus \(0 \le \sin h \le h\text{.}\) Since sine is odd, it follows that \(|\sin h| \le |h|\) whenever \(|h| \lt \pi/2\text{.}\) Given \(\varepsilon \gt 0\text{,}\) take \(\delta=\min\{\varepsilon,\pi/2\}\text{.}\) If \(|h| \lt \delta\text{,}\) then
\begin{equation*} |\sin h-\sin 0|=|\sin h|\le |h|\lt\varepsilon. \end{equation*}
Hence sine is continuous at \(0\text{.}\)
